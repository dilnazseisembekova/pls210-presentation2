The proposed causal mechanism is that US academic affiliation signals greater perceived institutional reputation, which students may use as a credibility cue, resulting in higher perceived credibility ratings for otherwise identical political research.

WHAT HAS CHANGED SINCE RESEARCH PROPOSAL 1?

MY HYPOTHESIS

H1: Political research attributed to US-based academic institutions will receive higher perceived credibility ratings than identical research attributed to Kazakhstan-based academic institutions.

EXPECTATIONS

Expectation 1 - Research credibility (H1):
I expect NU Political Science students to assign higher credibility ratings to research attributed to US-based academic institutions than to identical research attributed to Kazakhstan-based institutions.
Expectation 2 - Perceived academic reputation (H2):
I expect students to associate US-based academic institutions with greater academic reputation and authority than Kazakhstan-based institutions.
Expectation 3 - Reputation and credibility:
I expect students who perceive an academic affiliation as more reputable to also evaluate the associated research as more trustworthy and reliable.

Conceptualization

COUNTRY OF ACADEMIC INSTITUTIONAL AFFILIATION

Definition: The country in which the academic institution identified as the researcher's institutional affiliation is located.

PERCEIVED RESEARCH CREDIBILITY

Definition: The extent to which an individual judges academic research and its findings to be trustworthy and academically reliable.

PERCEIVED INSTITUTIONAL REPUTATION

Definition: An individual's assessment of an academic institution's standing and recognition within the academic community.

OPERATIONALIZATION
Country of academic institutional affiliation

• Operationalization: Participants are randomly assigned to one of two experimental conditions: The US VS Kazakhstan

• Variation: Two categories, US and Kazakhstan.

• Scale: Categorical

• Construct validity: Strong; the label changes only the country of affiliation. does not measure the researcher’s nationality or the quality of the institution.

• Reliability: High procedural consistency is achievable because the same standardized label can be shown to everyone assigned to each condition.

Perceived research credibility

• Operationalization: Students’ ratings of 1) research trustworthiness and 2) academic reliability

• Variation: 1–10 for each item

• Scale: Ordinal

• Content validity: The indicators directly measure the two attributes included in our definition: trustworthiness and academic reliability.

• Reliability: Use identical wording and response scales across both experimental conditions. Assess consistency between the two credibility indicators.

Perceived institutional reputation

• Operationalization: Students’ rating of the academic standing associated with the affiliation

• Variation: 1–10 for each item

• Scale: Ordinal

• Validity: Measures institutional reputation, but students may interpret generic affiliation labels differently.

• Reliability: Standardized wording improves consistency, but a single item prevents internal consistency testing.

Limitations

◎What the Design Can and Cannot Show
✓ Can show
A causal effect of academic affiliation country (US vs. Kazakhstan) on perceived research credibility and institutional reputation, within the experiment.
✕ Cannot show
Long-term effects, how exposure in real courses shapes credibility, or whether the findings generalize beyond NU Political Science students.
▥Key Threats to Conclusions
External validity
Convenience sampling from NU limits generalizability to other students and institutions.
Construct validity
Credibility and reputation are subjective judgments that may be interpreted differently by participants.
Demand effects
Participants might guess the study’s purpose and adjust their ratings accordingly.
⚠Overall Limitation
In sum, the experiment provides credible evidence of a short-term causal effect of affiliation cues, but its conclusions are limited by questions of generalizability, the measurement of credibility and reputation, and potential demand effects.
